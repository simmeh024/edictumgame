Privacy notice

Last updated 31 October 2026

In short. Edictum keeps what it needs to run the game: your player ID, your nation, your posts, support tickets, save diagnostics, the IP address you last played from (for 90 days) and, if you link one, your Google, Discord or email sign-in. Your nation and posts are public. There are no adverts, and Google Analytics counts your visits only if you allow it. You can delete your account and game data from the account menu; staff audit records remain as described below.

Who is responsible

Edictum (edictumgame.com) is a free online game run by Pascal Slettenaar, a private individual in Mexico, who is responsible for your personal data (the responsable under Mexico's Federal Law on the Protection of Personal Data Held by Private Parties, the LFPDPPP). Address: La Querencia 302-17, 21000 Aguascalientes, Aguascalientes, Mexico. Write to p.slettenaar@gmail.com with any question or request.

What the game keeps

  • Your account. When you open the game you get a guest account straight away: a random player ID, kept by Firebase Authentication. If you link a Google account, it also keeps the email address, name and profile picture address that Google shares, and your Google account ID, so that you can sign in on any device. If you link a Discord account, the game keeps your Discord account ID and display name, and which player ID they belong to; it asks Discord for nothing else (not your email address, servers or messages). If you link an email address, Firebase Authentication keeps that address and sends the sign-in links to it.
  • Your nation. Everything you create in the game: your nation's name, flag, motto and description, its statistics, land, cities, laws and history, your messages to other nations, your passport (its design, and the entry stamps you collect by opening other nations’ pages: which nations, and when), and when you last played.
  • Your posts on the region message boards, with the time and your player ID.
  • Reports. If you report another player, the report keeps your player ID, what you reported and why, and any note you add. Only the moderators see it.
  • Support tickets. If you create a support ticket on the Help page, it keeps your player ID, your nation's name and region, what you wrote, the email address if you choose to give one, and the team's answer. Only you and the game's team can read it, and the email address is used only to answer that ticket.
  • Moderation. If a moderator hides or removes your nation, asks you to give it a new name, or bans your account, the game keeps that decision and the reason. The game's admins can also keep private notes about your account for moderation, for example about an earlier warning. Only they can see these notes.
  • Save diagnostics. To investigate saving problems, staff can see your player ID, region, a Desktop/Mobile label, session status, save revision, pending-save flag, error code, conflict count and last report time. A random session identifier is kept in session storage. These reports contain no message text or email address.
  • Staff audit history. The game records admin actions, the acting admin's player ID, the target player or region, time, reason and outcome. Only staff can read this history.
  • The address you last played from. Each time you open the game, its server records the IP address your browser connects from, with the time, next to your player ID. Only the latest address is kept: a new one replaces the one before. Only the game's admins can see it, when they look into a player for moderation, for example to recognise a banned player who comes back. It is never shown to other players, and it is not used for anything else.
  • Technical data. The servers that deliver the game see your IP address and browser details when you load the page or sign in, and keep them in logs for a short time for security and to stop abuse.
  • Visit statistics, only if you allow them. The game asks once whether it may use Google Analytics, and only if you choose Allow does your browser load it. It then records which pages of the game you open (by their name, such as Edicts or Map, never with your player ID or your nation) and when, the site you came from, your type of device, browser, operating system, screen size and language, and roughly where you are (country and city, which Google works out from your IP address; Google Analytics does not keep the address). It keeps a random identifier in two cookies, _ga and _ga_…, for up to two years, to recognise a returning browser. Google's advertising features and Google signals are switched off. If you choose No thanks, nothing is loaded from Google Analytics.

  • Storage in your browser. The game keeps your nation, your settings, your sign-in and your answer about visit statistics in your browser's local storage, session storage and IndexedDB. The game needs these to work. Apart from the Google Analytics cookies above, set only if you allow visit statistics, it uses no cookies, web beacons or other technologies to track you, and nothing to show adverts.
  • Guest recovery and political actions. The server keeps only a hash of your one-time guest recovery code, not the code itself. A non-secret issuance marker prevents replacement codes; that marker may remain after account deletion. Assembly votes, proposals and leadership are protected server records displayed in the game. Temporary save approvals expire after two minutes. Hashed-IP recovery attempt counters expire after two hours; automatic expiry may take additional processing time.

The game asks for no sensitive personal data (such as health, beliefs or origin). Please do not put any personal information in your nation's texts or your posts: they are public.

What it is used for

To run the game for you, to answer your support tickets, and to keep the game safe and fair (reports, moderation, the address you last played from, and logs). These purposes are all needed for the game to work. The one other purpose (a secondary purpose, finalidad secundaria) is visit statistics, to see how many people play and how the game is used, so that it can be improved: they happen only if you allow them, and saying no changes nothing in the game. There is no marketing, advertising or profiling, and the data is never sold. For players in the European Union, the legal bases are the agreement in the terms (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR), the game's legitimate interest in keeping it safe (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) and, for visit statistics, your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR).

What other players see

Your nation (its name, flag, motto, description, statistics, land, history and passport) and your board posts are public: every player, and anyone who looks at the game's data, can see them. Your email address, your Google or Discord account, your IP address, your reports, your support tickets and the admins' notes about you are not public.

Who processes it, and transfers

The game runs on Google Firebase (Hosting, Authentication and Cloud Firestore), which Google provides as a processor (encargado) under its data processing terms; Google only processes the data to run the game. The game's database is in the European Union (Cloud Firestore, the eur3 locations in Belgium and the Netherlands), and Firebase Authentication and Google's delivery network may process data in other countries, including the United States. If you allow visit statistics, Google also processes them for the game in Google Analytics, as a processor under its data processing terms, which may also be in the United States. Your data is not shared with anyone else, unless a competent authority requires it by law. When you link a Google account, Google shares your details with the game under Google's privacy policy; when you link a Discord account, Discord shares your account ID and name under Discord's privacy policy. Sign-in links for an email address are sent by Firebase Authentication. The game's fonts are served from edictumgame.com itself, so no other company sees your visit.

How long it is kept

  • Your account, nation and posts: until you delete them. A nation that has not been played for 250 days becomes dormant and disappears from other players' view, but is kept so that you can wake it.
  • Reports: until a moderator has dealt with them, then they are deleted.
  • Support tickets: deleted automatically 90 days after they are closed. You can ask for one to be deleted sooner.
  • Save diagnostics: expire seven days after the last report, or are removed when you delete your account. Automatic expiry may take additional processing time.
  • The IP address you last played from: deleted automatically 90 days after it was last recorded, or when you delete your account. Automatic expiry may take additional processing time.
  • The admins' notes about your account: until an admin deletes them, or until you delete your account.
  • Staff audit history: retained as a record of staff actions, including after a player's account is deleted. It cannot be edited or deleted from the game.
  • Backups: the database is backed up daily (kept 14 days) and can be restored to any moment of the last 7 days. Anything you delete is gone from the backups within 14 days.
  • Visit statistics: Google Analytics keeps the detailed records for at most 14 months. Its cookies stay in your browser for up to two years, unless you turn visit statistics off or clear them.

Deleting everything, and withdrawing consent

Open the account menu (at the foot of the side bar, or at the top of the founding page) and choose Delete my nation and account. That deletes your nation, every post you wrote, your support tickets, the address you last played from, the admins' notes about you and your account, with its links to Google, Discord or your email address, at once, and is the simplest way to withdraw your consent. If you play as a guest and your guest sign-in is too old to be removed, an empty guest ID without any data may remain in Firebase Authentication.

To withdraw your consent to visit statistics, choose Turn off in the account menu or above: the game stops sending them and deletes the Google Analytics cookies in this browser.

Your rights (ARCO and more)

You may access the data the game keeps about you, have it rectified, have it cancelled (deleted), and oppose its use; players in the European Union may also restrict its use and receive it in a portable form. Most of this you can do in the game. Otherwise, write to p.slettenaar@gmail.com with your name, your player ID (shown in the account menu), the right you want to exercise and what it concerns. You will get an answer within 20 business days, and a decision in your favour is carried out within 15 business days after that.

If you are not satisfied, you may complain to the data protection authority: in Mexico, the Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno; in the European Union, the authority of the country where you live.

Children

Edictum is not meant for children. If you are under 18, play only with your parent's or guardian's permission.
